EXHIBIT 00

Why this isn't a classification problem

Every "is this transaction fraudulent" model shares the same blind spot: it looks at one row and asks whether that row is suspicious. A farmed account is built specifically to pass that test.

What a row-classifier sees

Account #4,812: real-looking phone number. Plausible first order. KYC pending, like a third of all new signups. No single field trips a rule. Score: low risk. Thirteen more accounts just like it, scored the same way, one at a time, never compared to each other.

What the graph sees

Those same thirteen accounts share one device fingerprint. They signed up across 66 hours. Each claimed a referral bonus within six hours of creation, then went silent. Order values are identical to the rupee. None of that is visible in any single row — it only exists across the thirteen.

EXHIBIT 01

Six stages, deterministic until the last one

Every stage below is graph algorithms and explicit thresholds — no model, nothing learned, nothing that can't be traced back to a specific edge or number. An LLM only ever sees what stage five already decided, and only to write it up.

STAGE 1

Build the entity graph

Accounts become nodes. An edge forms wherever two accounts share a device_fingerprint_id, an instrument_hash, an IP subnet, or a referral link — weighted by how rare that overlap is to happen honestly.

STAGE 2

Hard-signal clustering

Connected components on the shared-device and shared-instrument edges alone. Two strangers legitimately sharing a payment instrument is rare enough that this signal is treated as near-certain on its own.

STAGE 3

Soft-signal clustering

Weighted community detection (Louvain) over the full graph — the only stage that can catch a ring connected by nothing but IP overlap and referral-chain timing. This is deliberately the harder case, and it's scored separately, not blended into one headline number.

STAGE 4

Score every candidate cluster

Signup-burst tightness, order-value templating, the claim-then-dormant pattern, post-signup engagement. Still zero models — every number here is a direct calculation over timestamps and amounts.

STAGE 5

Filter out the confounders

Explicit rules that actively look for evidence a dense cluster is legitimate — a household, a hostel, an organic referral tree — and suppress the flag when they find it. This stage's entire job is to leave real people alone.

STAGE 6

A bounded LLM writes the case

Only clusters that survive stage 5 reach an LLM, and it sees only the aggregate evidence above — never a name, phone number, or account ID. It writes a plain-English case and picks exactly one of three actions. It cannot ban, block, or move money.

The honest part

Soft-signal recall is lower on purpose — it's the case with no shared device or instrument, only IP overlap and referral timing, and it's the real test of the approach. It is not a scattered failure rate hiding behind an average: every single one of the 6 missed rings is the dataset's deliberately "hard mode" variant (slower claims, noisier order values), and both wrongly-flagged confounders are the deliberately "tight" household variant (a compressed, borderline-organic signup window). EXHIBIT 03

Every recommendation is bounded to three words

The system flags, explains, and recommends. A human executes. This isn't a policy choice made after the fact — there is no function in this codebase that bans an account, blocks a payment, or claws back a bonus.

HOLD_BONUS

Hold or reverse a pending referral bonus for this cluster, pending human review. Reserved for strong evidence — shared instrument or device, tight timing, templated spending, dormancy after claim.

MANUAL_REVIEW

Evidence is suspicious but not conclusive enough to touch money. A human analyst looks closer before anything happens.

NO_ACTION

Even after passing the deterministic filter, the model can still recommend doing nothing — its own secondary read of the evidence, not a rubber stamp of stage five.

A missed ring costs paid-out money. A false alarm costs a delayed payout pending review — never a punishment. That asymmetry is why stage five is tuned conservatively.
EXHIBIT 04

The LLM writes the case. It doesn't build it.

By the time a cluster reaches a model, five deterministic stages have already produced every fact in the case file. The model's only job is to explain it in one paragraph a human can act on in ten seconds.

EXHIBIT 05

What this doesn't claim

Stated plainly, because a demo that only shows its wins isn't trustworthy.

  • Real fraud rings are adversarial, not static. They evolve to evade exactly this kind of detection — these planted rings are necessarily more obvious than one built by someone who has seen a system like this before.
  • A ring sharing zero attributes — fully clean burner identities, no device, IP, or payment overlap — is structurally invisible to a graph-clustering approach. There's no edge to find.
  • LLM confidence scores are self-reported, not calibrated probabilities.
  • This is a bounded, synthetic demonstration — it has no access to Razorpay's actual cross-merchant network data, and makes no claim to outperform network-scale fraud infrastructure.
